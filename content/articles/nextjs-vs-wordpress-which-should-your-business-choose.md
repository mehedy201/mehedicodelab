"Should we use WordPress or Next.js?" is one of the most common questions I get from founders and marketing teams. The honest answer is that both are excellent, and the wrong choice usually comes from picking by hype instead of by need.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Choose **WordPress** when non-technical people edit content often and you want to launch quickly.
> - Choose **Next.js** when the site behaves like an application, or you need full control of speed and design.
> - A **headless** setup (WordPress for content, Next.js for the front end) gives both, at a higher cost.

## What each one is

**WordPress** is a content management system. You get an admin area, a huge plugin ecosystem and themes. It powers a very large share of the web.

**Next.js** is a React framework. You build the site in code, and it can pre-render pages for speed and search engines. Content comes from files, a database or a CMS of your choice.

## Side by side

| | WordPress | Next.js |
|---|---|---|
| Time to launch | Fast | Slower (custom build) |
| Editing by non-developers | Excellent, out of the box | Needs a CMS added |
| Speed ceiling | Good, needs care | Very high, by design |
| Custom logic (dashboards, accounts, workflows) | Possible, often awkward | Natural |
| Plugin and theme risk | Higher (updates, conflicts) | Lower surface area |
| Typical cost | Lower | Higher upfront |
| SEO | Strong | Strong, needs deliberate setup |

SEO is not a reason to choose either one. Both can rank well when the pages are fast, crawlable and useful.

## Choose WordPress when

- You run a blog, brochure site, local business site or WooCommerce store.
- Your team will publish and change pages every week.
- You need to launch within weeks on a sensible budget.

## Choose Next.js when

- You are building a web app, customer portal, marketplace or SaaS front end.
- You need custom interactions, animations or tight control over performance.
- Your design cannot be built cleanly inside a theme.

## The hybrid option: headless WordPress

Editors keep the WordPress admin they know. Visitors get a fast Next.js front end. It is a good fit for content-heavy brands that also want a custom experience. The trade-offs are two systems to host, more setup, and previews that need extra work.

## A simple way to decide

1. Who will update content, and how often?
2. Is the site mostly pages, or mostly features?
3. What is the budget for the first build, and for the next two years?
4. How much do speed and custom design matter to revenue?
